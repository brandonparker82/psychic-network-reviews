In-Depth Psychic Network Reviews, From Keen and Kasamba to Oranum and Beyond

The big networks you’ve probably already heard of aren’t the only option, and they’re not automatically the best one for you either.

We compare the large, well-known networks against smaller, boutique-style platforms on the same set of terms, so you can see where each one actually stands out and where it doesn’t.

Compare Networks Now

A psychic network is really a marketplace: hundreds of independent readers under one roof, with the network setting the pricing structure, the vetting bar, and the rules for what happens when a session goes badly. Two networks can look nearly identical on the surface and still work very differently once you’re actually paying for a reading.

Most comparisons stop at surface details, reader counts, star ratings, whether there’s an app. We go further and look at how each network actually operates once your card is on file: how new readers get approved, what a first-timer’s discount actually restricts, and how disputes get resolved when a reading disappoints you. Those details rarely make it into a homepage, but they’re the ones that shape whether you’d actually recommend a network to a friend.

This site exists to put that information in one place, compared fairly and updated as networks change their policies, so you’re picking based on how a service actually runs rather than how its homepage is written.

Our Process

How We Build a Fair Comparison Between Networks

Comparing networks fairly means running the same checks on a two-decade-old platform with thousands of readers and a boutique site with a dozen. Here’s the three-part process behind every comparison we publish.

1. We Map the Reader Pool

We look at how a network describes its vetting process for new readers, how large and varied its reader pool actually is across different specialties, and whether it’s easy to find a consistent reader to return to versus getting routed to whoever happens to be available. A network with thousands of readers isn’t automatically better if finding the right one feels like guesswork. We also note whether a network lets you filter by specialty at all, since that alone can make or break the experience of choosing someone.

2. We Price Out a Real Session

Introductory offers make almost every network look inexpensive at first glance, so we price out what a typical returning customer actually pays once that offer expires. We also check for less obvious costs, subscription add-ons, minimum session lengths, or credits that expire before you can use them.

3. We Test How Problems Get Resolved

Where possible, we look at how a network handles a customer who wasn’t satisfied with a session, whether that means requesting a credit, flagging a reader, or asking for a refund. How smoothly and fairly that process runs tells you more about a network’s real character than its marketing copy ever will. A network that makes this process easy to find and quick to resolve stands out, and one that buries it doesn’t get the benefit of the doubt.

The result is a comparison built on the same three questions for every network, big or small, so you’re looking at like-for-like information instead of marketing claims stacked against each other.

What a Network Reading Can Tell You

A reading through any network can offer a fresh perspective on a situation, a different way of framing a decision, or simply someone to talk a problem through with. Which reader and which format you choose changes the experience more than which network you picked.

What a reading, on any network, can’t do is replace medical, legal, or financial professionals, and no session should be treated as a substitute for that kind of advice. No network we cover here can guarantee a specific outcome from a reading, regardless of what its marketing implies, and a longer track record doesn’t change that.

With that in mind, the sections below cover some of the bigger structural questions worth understanding before you commit to a network at all.

Large Networks vs Small Boutique Sites

A large network gives you scale: more readers, more specialties, and typically a longer track record to check reviews against. A smaller boutique site trades that scale for a tighter, more curated reader pool.

Neither structure is automatically the better choice. It depends on whether you’d rather have more options to sort through or fewer options that have already been narrowed down for you. Some readers do best with a huge pool to search through, others do better letting a smaller, more curated site do that narrowing for them.

Good to ask

  • How many active readers does this network currently carry?
  • How is a boutique site’s smaller reader pool actually curated?
  • What happens if my preferred reader on a small site isn’t available?

Better asked elsewhere

  • Assume bigger automatically means better vetted.
  • Assume a smaller site is automatically more personal without checking.

First-Time Offers and What They Really Buy You

Nearly every network leads with some version of a steep first-session discount, and it’s easy to judge a whole platform by that one number. The actual test is what happens once the discount runs out.

A short discounted session is also rarely enough time for a reader to get into real depth on anything complicated, so treat it as a way to sample a reader’s style rather than a full reading. Think of it as a trial run, not a preview of exactly what every future session will feel like.

Before committing further, it’s worth understanding the regular per-minute or per-session rate, whether there are any subscription add-ons bundled in by default, and whether unused introductory minutes expire on a timer.

What Happens When a Reading Goes Wrong

Not every session lands well, and how a network responds when that happens tells you more about it than any five-star review does.

Some networks offer a straightforward credit or partial refund for a session you weren’t happy with. Others make you argue your case through several layers of support before anything gets resolved.

Good to ask

  • What’s your policy if I’m not satisfied with a reading?
  • How long does a credit or refund request usually take?
  • Can I flag a specific reader without losing access to the network?

Better asked elsewhere

  • Assume every network handles this the same way.
  • Skip reading the fine print on refund eligibility.

Pro tip: check a network’s refund policy before your first session, not after a bad one, since that’s usually the moment you’ll actually need it.

Network Formats

The Different Ways Networks Deliver a Reading

Beyond size and pricing, networks differ in the format a reading actually takes, and that format shapes the whole experience as much as the reader you end up with does.

Below, we cover five common formats you’ll run into while comparing networks: chat-first platforms, phone-first platforms, video-based sessions, email-reading services, and marketplace-style sites where you browse and pick a reader directly rather than being routed to one.

We compare networks within their own format rather than across formats, since a chat-first platform and a phone-first platform are solving different problems and shouldn’t be judged by the same yardstick. Some networks even offer more than one format, and we note that flexibility where it exists, since being able to switch formats with the same reader is a real convenience for some people.

Chat-First Networks: Reading at Your Own Pace

Chat-first networks route most sessions through typed conversation, which gives you time to think through your question and reread what a reader actually said, rather than processing everything in the moment.

We look at how these networks handle typing speed and response gaps, since a slow-typing reader can make a chat session feel dragged out even when the content is solid, and long silent gaps between messages can eat through paid minutes fast.

  • A paced, written conversation you can revisit later
  • A more private option if a phone call isn’t practical
  • Clear timestamps so you know exactly what you paid for
  • Follow-up questions handled without needing to rebook a call
Compare Chat-First Networks

Phone-First Networks: The Original Format

Phone-first networks are the format most people picture first, live conversation, real-time back-and-forth, and a reader who can respond to your tone as much as your words.

We check call quality, how clearly a network states its per-minute billing, and whether a reader tends to keep the conversation moving or pad the call with small talk to run up the clock.

  • A live conversation with real-time follow-up
  • A reader who can pick up on tone, not just words
  • Clear per-minute billing stated before the call connects
  • An option that doesn’t require a stable internet connection
Compare Phone-First Networks

Video Networks: A Face-to-Face Alternative

Video sessions sit between phone and in-person, giving you facial expressions and body language on top of everything a phone call already offers.

We look at how stable a network’s video infrastructure actually is, since a session that keeps dropping the connection undercuts even a genuinely skilled reader, and often eats up paid minutes while it reconnects.

  • A face-to-face reading without leaving home
  • Visual cues layered on top of a spoken reading
  • A format well suited to a longer, more involved session
  • The option to see a reader’s setup and materials directly
Compare Video Networks

Email-Reading Services: A Written Record You Keep

Email-based readings trade real-time interaction for a considered, written response you can save and revisit whenever you want, often days after you first sent your question.

We look at how much depth a reader actually delivers in writing, since a thin, generic reply is easy to disguise as thorough when there’s no back-and-forth to test it against, and we check how long a network actually takes to deliver the response it promised.

  • A detailed written reading you can reread anytime
  • No need to be available at a specific time
  • A good fit for a complex question that needs unpacking
  • A permanent record with no risk of misremembering what was said
Compare Email-Reading Services

Marketplace-Style Platforms: Choosing a Reader Directly

Marketplace-style platforms let you browse reader profiles, reviews, and specialties yourself instead of being routed to whoever’s next available, closer to shopping than being assigned.

We check how honest and current those profiles actually are, whether reviews look independently sourced, and how easy it is to find a reader again after a good session.

  • Full control over which reader you book
  • Profile details and reviews to compare before you commit
  • The ability to rebook a reader you liked previously
  • More responsibility on you to vet who you’re choosing

None of these five formats is universally better than the others. The right one depends on how you want to receive information and how much control you want over who delivers it, and that’s exactly why we compare within each format instead of forcing every network into one ranking.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a bigger network always a safer choice than a smaller one?

Not necessarily. Size can mean a longer track record and a larger reader pool, but a smaller network can offer tighter vetting and more consistency. We compare both on the same terms rather than assuming bigger wins.

Why do first-session discounts vary so much between networks?

Discounts are a marketing tool meant to get you in the door, and networks compete hard on that first number. We focus our comparisons on the regular, ongoing rate, since that’s what you’ll actually be paying long-term, well after the introductory offer has expired.

Can a network guarantee my reading will be accurate?

No. No network, large or small, can guarantee a specific outcome from a reading, and any comparison implying otherwise should raise a flag rather than build confidence.

How do you handle networks that change their pricing or policies?

We recheck networks on a rolling basis rather than treating a comparison as a one-time write-up, and update our findings when a network’s pricing or dispute process changes noticeably.

What if a network I’m considering isn’t covered here yet?

Let us know. We’re steadily expanding coverage, and a request for a specific network helps us prioritize which one to look at next.
